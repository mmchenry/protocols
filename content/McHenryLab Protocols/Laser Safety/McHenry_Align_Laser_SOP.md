## PURPOSE

This SOP describes safe alignment procedures for laser systems in the McHenry lab. It covers
visible lasers (405–658 nm, including violet, green, and red) and near-infrared (808 nm) laser
alignment. Because these two wavelength ranges involve different hazard profiles and physical
access requirements, they are covered in separate sections below.

> **Most laser accidents occur during alignment. Read this SOP in full before
> performing any alignment procedure. Never deviate from these procedures without
> PI approval.**

---

## SCOPE

This SOP applies to alignment of Class 2 through Class 4 laser systems,
specifically:

- **Visible lasers:** 405 to 658 nm, 1 mW to 1500 mW, CW and Pulsed
- **Near-IR lasers:** ≥800 nm, 1000 W to 1700 W, CW and Pulsed

For general laser operation outside of alignment, refer to the McHenry "Laser General Use SOP".

## GENERAL REQUIREMENTS (ALL ALIGNMENTS)

The following requirements apply to every alignment regardless of wavelength. Complete
these before beginning either the visible or IR alignment procedures.

### Training and Authorization

- You must have completed UCI EHS General Laser Safety Training before performing
  any alignment
- You must have received lab-specific alignment training from the PI or designated trainer
- Alignment of Class 3b or Class 4 lasers requires explicit PI authorization
- Avoid performing alignment alone — use the buddy system, especially outside normal
  working hours

### Area Preparation

- [ ] Confirm only authorized personnel are present in the lab area
- [ ] Post DANGER signs at lab entrance; activate exterior warning lights if applicable
- [ ] Lock or close the lab door
- [ ] Close all curtains, doors, and access areas around the laser area
- [ ] Clear the optical table and surrounding area of:
  - Combustible materials
  - Unnecessary reflective objects (monitors, glass items, structural beams)
  - Trip hazards
- [ ] When passing a laser through optics, have a pre-drawn beam path diagram ready before starting — diagram all planned optic positions and beam routes before energizing any laser
- [ ] When aligning a laser sheet, pre-mark the desired position of the laser sheet

### Equipment Preparation

- [ ] Gather all required equipment before starting:
  - Beam stops and blocks
  - Power meter / detector
  - Beam profiling system or camera
  - Camera capable of detecting laser light at low power
  - (For >800 nm lasers) IR viewer cards or IR camera
  - Non-reflective tools — no bare metal screwdrivers, wrenches, or tweezers in the beam path
- [ ] Confirm all optics and mounts are secured to the table
- [ ] Place beam stops behind all optics that will be adjusted to catch errant reflections
- [ ] Confirm all beam stops used with Class 4 lasers are fire-resistant

### Personal Preparation

- [ ] Remove all jewelry and reflective badges
- [ ] Don correct laser safety eyewear for the specific wavelength being aligned (see General Use SOP Appendix B for eyewear inventory)
- [ ] Inspect eyewear before use — check for cracks, pitting, discoloration, or frame damage

### Power Reduction for Alignment

Always align at the lowest possible power level. Increase power only when absolutely
required for final steps, image through the camera whenever possible at higher power.

- Perform coarse/rough alignment with the beam blocked whenever possible
- **CW lasers:** reduce to minimum stable power output
- **Pulsed lasers:** use single-pulse firing mode when practical; reduce power
<!-- - Use ND filters, beam splitters, or beam dumps to further reduce beam power during alignment where practical -->
<!-- - If initial alignment was done at reduced power but final verification requires full power,
  switch to full-power-rated eyewear before proceeding -->

### Beam Control (All Alignments)

- Confine all beams to the optical table or benchtop at all times
- Keep all beam paths below eye level for both seated and standing users
- Never direct beams upward or across walkways
- If any beam path changes elevation (+Z), label that section clearly and be extra alert for vertical reflections
- Enclose beam paths as much as practically possible
- Power down the laser or close the shutter (if applicable) whenever making coarse adjustments to optics
- After completing all alignment steps, check for stray beams one final time before declaring alignment complete

## SECTION 1 — VISIBLE LIGHT ALIGNMENT (405–658 nm)

This section covers alignment of all visible laser systems in this lab operating between
405 nm and 658 nm. The operator is present in the room during this alignment. Visible
beams in this range span violet (405 nm) through visible red (658 nm). The natural blink
reflex provides some protection at Class 2 power levels only. At Class 3b and Class 4 levels, injury can occur before you can blink.

> **Note on 405 nm:** The violet beam can appear dim and may be harder to see clearly
> with the naked eye. Always use a beam viewing card to confirm beam position when
> working with 405 nm lasers.

### Viewing the Visible Beam (405–658 nm)

- Visible beams in the 405–658 nm range can be seen directly on diffuse surfaces (white card, beam viewing card, etc.). Beam brightness and color will vary by wavelength. 405 nm (violet) may appear dim — always confirm beam position with a viewing card
- Direct (intrabeam) viewing is prohibited at all times, even with safety eyewear
- When using a viewing card to locate the beam, hold the card at a slight angle so you are viewing the diffuse reflection, not the specular reflection
- Reach into the beam path slowly and deliberately — adjust the optic so the beam strikes the card just in front of the optic surface
- Be aware that some viewing cards can produce specular reflections, smoke, or burn under higher-power beams

### 635 nm Alignment Procedure

**Pre-alignment checklist** (complete all items in the General Requirements section first)

**Step-by-step beam alignment:**

1. Complete all general area, equipment, and personal preparation checklists above
2. Place a beam block behind the first optic before moving to the next position
3. Confirm laser shutter is closed, if applicable, or the cap is removed; insert key and power on the laser
4. Set output to minimum power — [lab-specific minimum power setting]
5. Before opening the shutter, verbally announce "laser on" to anyone else in area
6. Open the shutter, if applicable, only when you are ready to proceed with the first alignment step
7. Place a beam viewing card or beam stop at the first optic position in the planned
   beam path
8. Identify the beam position on the card
9. Adjust the optic to the desired position; close the shutter or power off the laser between adjustments
10. Check for stray beams (errant reflections from mirrors, polarizers, lenses) at each step before proceeding
11. Place a beam block behind the next optic to adjust
12. Progress down the optical path one component at a time, repeating steps 6-10
13. After all components are aligned, open the shutter and do a final check for stray beams across the entire beam path
14. Close the shutter; reduce power to minimum; power off the laser; remove key

**Step-by-step Laser sheet alignment:**

1. Complete all general area, equipment, and personal preparation checklists above
2. Mark the desired sheet placement in at least two places (ex. with measured tape and/or pen marks on the side of experimental tank)
3. Confirm laser shutter is closed, if applicable, or the cap is removed; insert key and power on the laser
4. Set output to minimum power — [lab-specific minimum power setting]
5. Before opening the shutter, verbally announce "laser on" to anyone else in area
6. Open the shutter, if applicable, only when you are ready to proceed with the first alignment step
7. Adjust laser to the desired position; close the shutter or power off the laser between adjustments
8. Check for stray beams (errant reflections from mirrors, polarizers, lenses) before proceeding
9. Once confident that the sheet is aligned, open the shutter and do a final check for stray beams across the entire beam path
10. Close the shutter; reduce power to minimum; power off the laser; remove key

### After Visible Laser Alignment

- [ ] Laser is powered off and key removed
- [ ] Shutter is closed, cap is on, if applicable
- [ ] All stray beams have been confirmed absent
- [ ] Eyewear returned to designated storage

## SECTION 2 — NEAR-INFRARED ALIGNMENT (808 nm)
**See Appendix A - Laser Inventory, IR lasers**

This section covers alignment of the 808 nm laser system(s).

> **CRITICAL: Beams >800 nm are completely invisible. Your eye's natural blink
> reflex will NOT protect you. There is no warning sensation before a retinal
> injury occurs.**

Because of the elevated hazard level of IR lasers, the operator does not remain
in the room while the IR laser is energized and the shutter is open. All active
beam steering and verification is performed remotely using IR sensitive cameras.


### Eyewear Requirements — IR Lasers

Laser safety eyewear rated for 808 nm at the appropriate OD is **required** any time you
are in the room with the 808 nm beam path active, even with the shutter closed.

- Eyewear for 808 nm: [Model, OD value, storage location]

> **Important:** Eyewear rated for 635 nm is NOT necessarily adequate for 808 nm.
> Always verify OD coverage before entering the room. If unsure, contact the PI or
> UCI EHS LSO: 949-824-6200.

### Viewing the 808 nm Beam

Direct (intrabeam) viewing is strictly prohibited. The following tools must be used to
locate and verify the 808 nm beam:

- **IR viewer cards / phosphor cards:** Hold at a slight angle to see diffuse
  fluorescence. Be aware cards can smoke or burn under higher-power beams. Reach
  slowly and deliberately into the beam path.
- **IR camera / beam profiling camera:** Preferred method for remote real-time beam
  monitoring during active alignment

### 808 nm Alignment Procedure

The 808 nm alignment is performed in two phases:

**Phase 1 — Mechanical Pre-Alignment (laser off, in-room)**

All physical positioning of optics is done with the laser off and key removed before
leaving the room.

1. Complete all general area, equipment, and personal preparation checklists above
2. Confirm the laser is powered off and the key is removed
3. Using the pre-drawn beam path diagram, position all optics, mounts, and beam stops
   on the optical table
4. Install the IR camera or beam profiling system at the target location — confirm the
   camera feed is accessible from outside the room
5. Place IR viewer cards at key checkpoints along the beam path if needed for
   intermediate verification
6. Do a final in-room sweep: confirm all optics are secured, all beam stops are in place,
   and the table is clear of combustibles and reflective items
7. Don 808 nm eyewear; do a final visual check of the room
8. Remove eyewear and exit the room; lock the door

**Phase 2 — Active Beam Alignment (remote, outside the room)**

All beam steering and verification is done from outside the room using the camera feed.

1. From outside the room, insert key and power on the 808 nm laser
2. Set power to minimum
3. Announce "808 nm laser on" to your buddy / anyone in the hallway
4. Open the shutter — confirm the beam is visible on the IR camera feed
5. Using remote adjustment tools [describe your specific mechanism — e.g., motorized
   mirror mounts, manual fine-adjust knobs accessible from outside, etc.], steer the
   beam to the first target position
6. Close the shutter remotely before making any in-room access (see below)
7. Check the camera feed for stray beams at each alignment step

## EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

### Suspected Eye or Skin Exposure During Alignment

1. Stop alignment immediately — close shutter, power off laser
2. Do not rub the eye(s)
3. Notify the PI immediately
4. Seek medical evaluation promptly — do not wait for symptoms. Retinal injuries from
   808 nm radiation cause no immediate pain
5. PI must notify UCI EHS / LSO: **949-824-6200**
6. Campus Emergency: **911** (campus phone) or **949-824-5222** (cell)

> **For 808 nm:** If you are unsure whether an exposure occurred — treat it as an
> exposure and seek evaluation. There is no safe assumption with invisible radiation.

### Fire

1. Close shutter and power off laser if safe to do so
2. Use fire extinguisher for small, contained fires if trained to do so
3. Fire extinguisher location: McGaugh Hall rm. 5346
4. Evacuate and call 911 if not immediately controlled

## ALIGNMENT RULES — QUICK REFERENCE

- Never perform alignment without PI authorization
- Never align alone — use the buddy system
- Always use lowest practical power during alignment
- Always close the shutter between optics adjustments
- Always place beam blocks behind optics before adjusting them
- Check for stray beams at every step and at the end
- **405–658 nm (visible):** Operator in room; eyewear required; verify eyewear OD is
  correct for the specific wavelength in use before starting; use viewing cards for beam
  location; note that 405 nm may appear dim — always confirm with a card
- **808 nm:** Operator OUT of room when shutter is open; use IR camera for remote
  verification; never assume the beam is off
- Never bypass any interlock during alignment
- If anything unexpected happens — close the shutter first, then investigate
- Log every alignment session in the lab logbook
